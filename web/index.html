WHAT THE SOLVER IS DOING

A curve feels the field around it.

The reported travel cost adds local cost times distance along the drawn route. The default method descends an auxiliary energy. In the continuous model, stationary curves of this energy satisfy the path ODE; the sampled curve approximates it. Energy can fall while travel cost briefly rises; they answer related but distinct numerical questions.

Energy descent accepts steps that lower energy. Damped Newton accepts steps that reduce the ODE residual and makes no energy-decrease promise. While you hold a point, that vertex is fixed. The solver seeks stationarity only at the other vertices, so the route behaves as two constrained pieces meeting at your waypoint. Release it to optimize the whole route again. A small diagnostic is evidence of numerical stationarity, not proof of a globally cheapest route.
