THE MATHEMATICS

The path equations.

A route pays more where the landscape costs more. These are the equations the playground solves.

The objective

Let q(t) = (x(t), y(t)) be a route with fixed endpoints, and let c(x, y) > 0 be its local cost. Its weighted length is

J[q] = ∫₀¹ c(q(t)) ‖q′(t)‖ dt

We use the related energy

E[q] = ∫₀¹ c(q(t))² ‖q′(t)‖² dt

The energy selects constant weighted speed along a stationary curve; continuous minimizing routes have the same shapes for both objectives.

Apply Euler–Lagrange

For smooth positive c, substitute L = c²(x′² + y′²) into d/dt(∂L/∂x′) − ∂L/∂x = 0 and its y equation. Primes are derivatives in t; cₓ and cᵧ are spatial derivatives. Its useful partial derivatives are

∂L/∂x = 2ccₓ(x′² + y′²), ∂L/∂x′ = 2c²x′
∂L/∂y = 2ccᵧ(x′² + y′²), ∂L/∂y′ = 2c²y′

After simplifying, the route satisfies

x″ = [cₓ(y′² − x′²) − 2cᵧx′y′] / c
y″ = [cᵧ(x′² − y′²) − 2cₓx′y′] / c

Try it

The endpoints stay fixed while the interior points move to approximately satisfy these ODEs. A stationary route is not necessarily the cheapest route.
